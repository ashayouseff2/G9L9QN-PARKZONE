# Park Zone — Clear parking decisions

**Presenter:** Yousef Asha  
**Presentation:** [PWMD source](presentation.md)

## Short Summary

Park Zone is a parking-information concept for drivers in unfamiliar areas. It addresses the difficulty of understanding parking-area type, eligibility, operating hours, restrictions, and payment conditions before leaving a car.

## Explanation of the Core

The project began with two interviews with drivers in Hungary. Both participants described difficulty finding parking payment information. One participant also described difficulty understanding parking rules and prices because of a language barrier. The findings are preliminary and come from a small sample.

The design direction is to show the parking-area type and eligibility before payment. A driver first selects or confirms a location, reviews the area result, and then reads the applicable conditions. Payment is presented only when the area is public paid parking. When the location or rules cannot be verified, the interface should explain the uncertainty rather than imply that parking is permitted.

The clickable prototype was reviewed by three separate participants. Feedback was generally positive, although some steps felt complex and official information was identified as important for trust. The review was qualitative; formal task-completion times and individual success rates were not collected.

The current demonstration uses example parking details. It is not connected to live municipal data or a payment service.

## Main Takeaways

- Drivers need understandable parking information before leaving the car.
- Eligibility and restrictions should be visible before payment.
- Missing or uncertain information must be clearly communicated.
- Official sources are important for trust and future validation.

## Concepts Learned

- **Information architecture:** organizing information and navigation so users can find what they need.
- **User flow:** the sequence of steps a user follows to complete a task.
- **Eligibility:** whether a user is allowed to use a parking area under its rules.
- **Usability testing:** observing people using a design to identify difficulties and opportunities for improvement.
